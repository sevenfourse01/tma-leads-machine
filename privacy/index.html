4. Enquiry assessments of dental practices (Mercer)

What it is. We assess how a dental practice handles a routine new-patient enquiry: whether it is acknowledged, how long a reply takes and whether the question is answered. An enquiry is sent by a fictional enquirer (a "persona") and is always routine and non-clinical. A persona never describes symptoms, never claims an emergency, never gives patient details and never accepts or holds an appointment.

What is live today. Website enquiry forms only, in a small pilot, and only for a practice whose owner or manager has agreed in writing and has told the practice's staff that an assessment will take place. We do not run unannounced assessments. Before any began we would consult the Information Commissioner's Office and update this notice. Telephone and email assessments are built but not switched on, and this notice will be updated before they are.

Whose data, and what. The assessment is of the practice, not of any person. It still touches personal data of practice staff who answer:

  • the practice's public details (name, website, published contact address, CQC register entry);
  • the name, role and contact details of the person who signs the practice's agreement, and the agreement itself;
  • the name and work email address of whoever replies, and what they write;
  • screenshots of the practice's public enquiry page as we submitted it;
  • times: when the enquiry was sent and when a reply arrived;
  • a score for the practice built from those facts.

We do not seek special category data and the enquiry gives a practice no reason to send any. We refuse forms that ask for patient details, such as a date of birth or medical history.

Where the data comes from. The CQC public register, the practice's own website, the practice's agreement with us, and the practice's replies.

Why, and the lawful basis. To measure and report enquiry handling so practices can improve it. The lawful basis is our legitimate interests and those of the practice that asked for the assessment (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). The practice's agreement is a condition we set before any enquiry is sent; it is not the lawful basis for staff data. We hold the details of the person who signs the agreement to perform that agreement (Article 6(1)(b)).

Use of results. A practice's results are reported to that practice. They are not used for marketing.

Automated activity. Enquiries are sent by software, and replies are sorted and timed by software, in future with the help of an AI model for wording that simple rules cannot read. Scores are calculated by fixed rules. A person reviews anything the software is unsure of, and a person approves every practice, every enquiry text and every batch before it is sent. Nothing here makes a decision with legal or similarly significant effect on any individual. If a member of staff sincerely asks a telephone persona whether they are speaking to a test, a recording or an AI, it says so and ends the call.

Limits we keep. A practice receives only the enquiries its agreement allows. Staff names are removed from stored reply text where our software can find them. Reports quote by role, never by name.

How to stop. A practice can withdraw at any time, and any member of staff can object: reply STOP to any message from us, or email the privacy contact above. We stop contacting that practice, cancel anything pending and keep only the record needed to make sure we do not contact it again.
