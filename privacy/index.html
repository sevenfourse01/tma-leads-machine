4. Enquiry assessments of dental practices (Mercer)

What it is. We assess how a dental practice handles a routine new-patient enquiry: whether it is acknowledged, how long a reply takes and whether the question is answered. An enquiry is made by a named member of our staff, in their own name, and is always routine: it asks only what a private examination costs, whether the practice is taking new private patients and how to register. We never say we intend to register, never invent anything about ourselves, never describe symptoms, never give patient details and never accept or hold an appointment. If a practice offers one, the answer is "Thanks, I'll think about it. Please don't book anything." If anyone asks whether the enquiry is a test, or who is behind it, they are told the truth.

We tell you at the end of every contact. At the end of every answered telephone call, and in our reply to the first message a person sends us by email or through a website form, we say who we are, that the contact was an enquiry-handling assessment, that we may use the result to offer the practice our services, and how to object. The assessment ends at that point.

Two kinds of assessment. An initial assessment is unannounced: two or three enquiries to a practice that has not been told beforehand, by website form, by email, or by a telephone call made by a member of our staff. A full assessment of twenty enquiries is run only for a practice whose owner or manager has agreed in writing and told the practice's staff.

Telephone calls are made by a person, not by software. They are not recorded. The caller records only fixed facts (whether the call was answered and after how long, whether a price was given, whether an appointment was offered) and keeps no note of what was said.

Whose data, and what. The assessment is of the practice, not of any person. It still touches personal data of practice staff who answer:

  • the practice's public details (name, website, published contact address, CQC register entry);
  • the name, role and contact details of the person who signs the practice's agreement, and the agreement itself;
  • for an email or form reply, the work email address of whoever replies and what they write, held only until the reply has been read and the practice scored (hours, at most a few days); after that we keep the times and the yes-or-no facts, and the reply text, the writer's name and address are deleted;
  • screenshots of the practice's public enquiry page as we submitted it;
  • for a telephone call, the fixed facts above and nothing a member of staff said;
  • times: when the enquiry was sent and when a reply arrived;
  • a score for the practice built from those facts.

We do not seek special category data and the enquiry gives a practice no reason to send any. We refuse forms that ask for patient details, such as a date of birth or medical history.

Where the data comes from. The CQC public register, the practice's own website, the practice's agreement with us, and the practice's replies.

Why, and the lawful basis. To measure and report enquiry handling so practices can improve it, and to find practices that our services could help. The lawful basis is our legitimate interests in doing both (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). For a full assessment we also hold the details of the person who signs the agreement, to perform that agreement (Article 6(1)(b)).

Use of results, including to offer our services. We use a practice's results to decide whether to contact that practice about our services, and we may refer to the results when we do. That contact is direct marketing. You can object to it at any time and we will stop: reply STOP or email the privacy contact. We do not approach a practice until at least seven days after we have told it about the assessment, and never if it has objected in the meantime; a request to stop goes on one list that both the assessments and our sales contact use. Before an unannounced call we check the number against the Corporate and Telephone Preference Services, and we do not send an unannounced email or form enquiry to a sole trader or a partnership. A practice's results are given to that practice and are not published or sold, and our sales contact refers only to the practice's result, never to anything a member of staff said.

Automated activity. Enquiries are sent by software, and replies are sorted and timed by software, in future with the help of an AI model for wording that simple rules cannot read. Scores are calculated by fixed rules. A person reviews anything the software is unsure of, and a person approves every practice, every enquiry text and every batch before it is sent. Nothing here makes a decision with legal or similarly significant effect on any individual. If a member of staff sincerely asks a telephone persona whether they are speaking to a test, a recording or an AI, it says so and ends the call.

Limits we keep. For an initial assessment, at most one enquiry per channel per practice in seven days and four in thirty days. A full assessment follows the practice's agreement. For an initial assessment nothing a member of staff said or wrote is kept once the practice is scored, and reports carry no quotes. For a full assessment, staff names are removed from stored reply text where our software can find them, and reports quote by role, never by name.

How to stop. A practice can withdraw at any time, and any member of staff can object: reply STOP to any message from us, email stop@themissionautomation.com, or email the privacy contact above. We stop contacting that practice, cancel anything pending and keep only the record needed to make sure we do not contact it again.
