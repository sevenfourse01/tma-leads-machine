How an enquiry moves

Six steps and one branch.

Anything clinical or unclear leaves the machine and goes to the clinic's named responder. Everything else is handled by rules and approved wording, and a small model only if the clinic switches one on.

  1. SOURCES

    Three doors

    Website form, practice inbox, missed-call text-back.

  2. S1

    Capture

    One record per person, its source stamped, a named owner.

  3. S2

    Respond

    The approved acknowledgement inside five minutes. Nothing about treatment.

  4. SORT

    Cheapest check first

    Rules, then the keyword list. Clinical or unclear goes to a person.

  5. S3

    Booking

    Real availability, one booking per slot, confirmation and reminders.

  6. S4

    Follow-up

    An approved sequence until a reply, a booking, a decision or a stop.

  7. S5

    Outcome

    Attended, plan accepted, paid. Paid only with evidence.

  8. S6

    Review

    Friday counts for the clinic, the exceptions and one improvement.

  9. CLINICAL OR UNCLEAR

    The responder

    A named person at the clinic answers every clinical or unclear message and every real conversation. The system makes a task. It does not guess.

Daily alarm

Any enquiry in the last 24 hours without an acknowledgement and an owner, or a source that has gone dark, reaches Zach on Telegram. Counts only, never a name.

What does not move

Six rules, written into the code and the tests.

01

Nothing clinical, from a machine

No automated message says anything about treatment. A lint runs over every template and every sent message, and clinical or unclear enquiries go to the clinic's named responder.

02

Data stays where the clinic approves

No access before the processor agreement is signed. No enquiry text through US AI services. Our own records get counts only.

03

Three sources

The website form, the practice inbox and the missed-call text-back. Phone answering, WhatsApp and social inboxes are out.

04

Honest states

A task created is not a message sent. A booking, an accepted plan or an unpaid invoice is not a paying patient. Paid needs an amount, a reference, who marked it and where the payment is recorded.

05

One alarm from day one

A daily check that every enquiry in the last 24 hours has an acknowledgement and an owner, with any miss repaired the same day.

06

Each clinic sealed off

Its own config file, data folder and credentials, a written rollback and a named person for failures. The practice management system is left alone.
