How an enquiry moves

Six steps, one branch. Anything clinical or unclear leaves the machine and goes to the clinic's named responder.

  1. SourcesWebsite form, practice inbox, missed-call text-back. Nothing else.
  2. S1 CaptureOne record per person, with its source and a named owner.
  3. S2 RespondThe approved acknowledgement, inside five minutes. It says nothing about treatment.
  4. SortCheap checks first: rules, then the keyword list, then a small model only if it is switched on.
  5. S3 BookingReal availability, one booking per slot, confirmation and reminders.
  6. S4 Follow-upAn approved sequence until a reply, a booking, a decision or a stop.
  7. S5 OutcomeAttended, plan accepted, paid. Paid only with evidence.
  8. S6 ReviewFriday counts for the clinic, with the exceptions and one improvement.
  9. ResponderA named person at the clinic answers every clinical or unclear message and every real conversation. The system makes a task; it never guesses.

Daily alarm: any enquiry in the last 24 hours without an acknowledgement and an owner, or a source that has gone dark, pings Zach on Telegram. Counts only, never a name.

The rules that do not move

Nothing clinical, ever
No automated message says anything about treatment. Clinical or unclear messages go to the clinic's named responder, every time. A lint runs over every template and every sent message.
Data stays where the clinic approves
No access before the processor agreement is signed. No enquiry text through US AI services; the model is off until the data sheet names a UK or EU processor, and the system passes the whole test set without it. Our own records get counts only.
Three sources
The website form, the practice inbox and the missed-call text-back. Phone answering, WhatsApp and social inboxes are out.
Honest states
A task created is not a message sent. A booking, an accepted plan or an unpaid invoice is never a paying patient. Paid needs an amount, a reference, who marked it and where the payment is recorded.
One alarm from day one
A daily check that every enquiry in the last 24 hours has an acknowledgement and an owner, sent to Zach on Telegram, with any miss repaired the same day.
Each clinic sealed off
Its own config file, its own data folder, its own credentials, a written rollback and a named person for failures. Swappable AI behind one adapter. Uses the calendar and inbox the clinic already has; the practice management system is left alone.

Where it stands

Built 12 November 2026 against the brief of 9 November. What is true today, without rounding up.

  • The whole system runs end to end on the test clinic with the model switched off, and the 46 cases pass, including the clinical row at zero tolerance.
  • Messages go to a file outbox on the test clinic. The email, text and Telegram senders exist but have not been run against a live account.
  • The inbox source reads files; the live mailbox adapter is named but not wired. The form and missed-call receivers are HTTP endpoints, ready for a clinic's form and telephony webhook.
  • The message texts are working copy. Eric owns the words; swapping them does not change the pipeline or the tests.
  • No clinic data has been touched. Everyone on this page is invented.
