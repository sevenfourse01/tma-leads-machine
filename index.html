Where growth budgets go

You've paid for growth before.
What did the money buy?

Six ways businesses lose money to traditional growth providers, over and over. We watched them from the inside, then built The Mission so none of them happen here: we show you the arithmetic behind every pound before we spend it.

FAILURE 01

Volume sold as strategy

Ten thousand near-identical emails look like work. Replies fall, the domain burns, and the pipeline you were paying to fill quietly closes for the next year. You paid to lose future customers.

FAILURE 02

Templates wearing your name

"Hi {{first_name}}, loved what you're doing at {{company}}." Buyers bin these on sight, and every one still gets billed to you.

FAILURE 03

Channels that could never pay

Paid ads sold to a business whose customer value can't carry the cost of winning one, because ads are what the provider sells. Nobody runs that check, and you find out what it cost at renewal.

FAILURE 04

Activity dressed as results

A monthly deck of impressions, opens and reach, with revenue nowhere on it. The retainer buys an accounting of busyness.

FAILURE 05

Signed, then sidelined

Full attention until the contract, slow replies after it, and an exit priced in months of notice.

FAILURE 06

Their playbook, your budget

Aggressive scale pushed on a business that wanted steady, because the plan was written before you were. Your budget follows what they sell and your goals never come up.

The unglamorous part that decides everything

Four channels,
one standard of care.

Wherever your budget goes, the same discipline applies: built properly before anything sends, priced against your economics, and stopped the moment it stops paying. A channel run carelessly doesn't just underperform; it spends your money twice, once on the sends and once on the customers it costs you.

Email

Separate sending domains, warmed slowly, authenticated to the current Gmail and Yahoo bulk-sender requirements, with complaint rates watched daily. A burned domain means your invoices and quotes start landing in spam too: money lost twice. We protect the domain your business runs on by never sending from it.

LinkedIn

Decision-makers found where they already gather, approached at a human pace: manually throttled, so your profile is never risked by automation bans. Every touch drafted from a real signal, because a banned account and a burned network cost more than the leads were worth.

Content

Built from material you already have: calls, videos, the questions clients ask you. Platform-native, drafted in your voice, and compounding, so the spend keeps paying after the month it was spent in. It also warms every other channel: buyers check you exist before they reply.

Paid

Run only where your customer value clears the cost of winning one, tracked to revenue, and metered to the pound against your budget. When the numbers stop clearing, the spend stops. Paid is where the most money disappears fastest, so it gets the strictest gate.

Straight answers

FAQs

Is this software I log into?
It's a service. You do get a dashboard, and you're on it from day one, because that's where the work shows up, but nobody hands you a login and walks away. Our team runs the system, stands in every loop that needs judgement, and the dashboard is where you watch it happen and have your say.
How is this different from buying an AI SDR tool?
You're hiring us to run growth with you. Software products hand you a login and a monthly bill, then leave the strategy, the deliverability, the copy and the judgement to you. We do all of that, on a system we built, with people watching it. The output can look similar; who is accountable for it is the whole difference.
Do I run it, or do you?
We run it, with you. You set the goals and the budget, see the work as it's drafted, and have the final say on what represents you. The sourcing, writing, sending, measuring and adjusting is ours. And if you'd like to drive it yourself one day, we teach you how.
Do emails ever go out without a human seeing them?
No. Every message and every template is signed off by a named person before it can reach anyone, and nothing carries a sent status without a recorded approver and timestamp. There's no setting that turns that off.
How do I approve the work?
Weekly, mostly: copy and campaign changes land in one queue and we go through them together. Once a template is signed off, messages built from it send on that approval rather than asking again each time, and you can watch emails and SMS follow-ups going out as they happen. How much of that sign-off you keep and how much sits with us is your call, and plenty of clients move the line as trust builds.
How fast can you have this running?
You're on the dashboard immediately; that's where all of the growth work happens from day one. Then we build the machine with you, typically around three weeks depending on scope, and we agree the launch date on the call before anything is signed. When we're both ready, we run it; after that it's updates. Engagements open with a trial period and then run three, six or twelve months. The term matters because it feeds the prediction system: different kinds of outreach become viable over different horizons, with direct outreach paying sooner and content compounding over months.
Do I need a technical person or a tech team?
No. You describe what's costing you money in plain English; we do the engineering. Nothing on your side needs configuring.
What happens once the systems are live?
Each Monday you get what we changed and what we're testing against next, in plain English. Beyond that, access is the point: a line to us around the clock, a call whenever you want to schedule one, and messages answered by a person once you're a client, on your business phone for now. Calls run monthly as standard, weekly if you want them, and we'd suggest a faster loop in the early weeks while the system beds in. If a number drifts mid-week, we message you first.
Will it make mistakes? Could a message go out that isn't true about my business?
It's our job for that never to happen, and the system is built around it. We draft only from claims your own material supports, the knowledge base is written down in plain text we maintain rather than left inside a model, and anything a human didn't write is approved by one before it moves. We also run regular check-ups on the details that drift in any business (availability, offers, phone cover) so no message repeats something out of date.
Can I run it myself?
After the build period we'll teach you how, free of charge. Most clients keep the retainer for the continued improvement and monitoring; some take the wheel themselves. Either way the machine is built for your business, and you're never locked out of it.
What if the forecast says my numbers don't work?
We tell you before you've paid us, and we don't go ahead. You get our suggestions for how the numbers could be made to work, which sometimes means changes to the offer or the business itself. And if we're not confident we can help a company, we won't take it on at that time.
Is the return guaranteed?
No, and it would be silly to claim one. What we do instead is score it: you get the modelled probability, with the assumptions printed alongside, and we log every forecast so it can be marked against what happened. The public demo is the best guess we can give away free; the engine we run for clients goes further. Results land higher or lower than the central case, and we show you by how much as they do.
